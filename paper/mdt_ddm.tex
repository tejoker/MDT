\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A parametric network (Deep Diffusion Maps, DDM) could in principle improve Multi-view Diffusion
Trajectories (MDT)---whose embedding is the SVD of a product of per-view diffusion operators---along
four axes: \emph{accuracy}, \emph{cost}, \emph{out-of-sample} extension, and \emph{end-to-end}
trainability. We report a negative result. Accuracy is capped by a ceiling argument: a Gram-matching
network's global optimum is the truncated SVD itself (Eckart--Young), so it cannot exceed the SVD of
the same operator---confirmed empirically. On cost, a truncated SVD on the operator's sparse action
matches the network's accuracy at $\sim$300$\times$ less compute. On out-of-sample---DDM's actual
motivation---a single run appears to favour Nystr\"om, but over five seeds Nystr\"om and the encoder
\emph{tie within a large run-to-run variance}: out-of-sample is a wash. The lone axis no argument or
baseline forecloses, end-to-end geometry learning, fails in practice---an optimisation/conditioning
failure (the contrastive loss stalls and the operator spectrum flattens), not the collapse it
superficially resembles. Several
early apparent ``wins'' were artifacts of weak baselines---a poor single random trajectory, and dense
eigendecomposition where a truncated iterative SVD belongs---which we document explicitly, because
they manufacture progress that does not exist.
\end{abstract}

\section{What we tested}
An MDT operator is a length-$t$ product $\W=\W_t\cdots\W_1$, each step a convex mixture of per-view
row-stochastic transitions $\W_s=\sum_v a_{sv}\mathbf{P}_v$; its embedding is the truncated SVD of
$\W$. MDT is transductive and, with a dense product, costs $O(N^3)$. DDM offers a parametric
encoder. The combination is appealing: a network might give cheaper, out-of-sample, or more accurate
embeddings. We tested whether it does, and---crucially---\emph{against the baselines a referee would
demand}, not the convenient ones.

\paragraph{Four axes, and what closes each.}
A network's embedding can differ from the classical SVD on four axes, and each is closed by an
argument or by the correct baseline.
\emph{Accuracy} is closed by a ceiling: a network trained to match the embedding's Gram matrix,
$\lVert \mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^\top - \mathbf{G}\rVert^2$, has its global optimum at the truncated SVD
of $\mathbf{G}$ (Eckart--Young\footnote{$\mathbf{G}$ is symmetric PSD with eigendecomposition
$\mathbf{G}=\sum_l\sigma_l^2\mathbf u_l\mathbf u_l^\top$ ($\sigma_1^2\!\ge\!\sigma_2^2\!\ge\!\cdots$). As
$\mathbf{F}$ ranges over $N\times k$ matrices, $\mathbf{F}\mathbf{F}^\top$ ranges over all rank-$k$ PSD
matrices, and Eckart--Young gives $\min_{\mathrm{rank}\,k}\lVert \mathbf{M}-\mathbf{G}\rVert_F$ at
$\mathbf{M}^\star=\sum_{l\le k}\sigma_l^2\mathbf u_l\mathbf u_l^\top$, i.e.\ the top-$k$ truncated
eigendecomposition. Hence the optimal $\mathbf{F}^\star=\mathbf U_{:,1:k}\,\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1:k})$
up to a right orthogonal rotation---exactly the SVD embedding. A parametric $\mathbf F=f_\theta(x)$ can
approach this optimum but, being a subset of all $N\times k$ matrices, can never beat it.}), so it
cannot exceed the SVD of the same operator.
\emph{Cost:} the correct baseline is \textbf{truncated \texttt{svds}} on the operator's sparse action
(a \texttt{LinearOperator} applying $\W$ by sparse matrix--vector products, never forming $N\times N$).
\emph{Out-of-sample:} the correct baseline is \textbf{Nystr\"om} extension. Note \texttt{svds} produces
only a table of values over the training rows---it cannot map a new point---so it is not a competitor
here; Nystr\"om is the SVD's own out-of-sample extension.
\emph{End-to-end:} making the embedding a trainable layer under a downstream objective is the one axis
no baseline forecloses---but it fails in practice (\S\ref{sec:collapse}).
A \textbf{features-only} $k$-means control isolates how much the operator contributes at all.

\section{Setup: what each scenario changes}
\paragraph{Baselines.}
DDM (single view) learns an embedding by Gram-matching to a \emph{fixed} spectral target,
\begin{equation}
\min_{\Gamma}\ \big\lVert \Pii^{1/2}\Gamma^\top\!\Gamma\,\Pii^{1/2}
-(\A^{2t}-\sqrt{\bp}\sqrt{\bp}^\top)\big\rVert_F^2,
\end{equation}
with $\A=\mathbf D^{-1/2}\mathbf K_\alpha\mathbf D^{-1/2}$ symmetric and $\bp$ its stationary
distribution. MDT (multi-view) instead builds the \emph{asymmetric} operator $\W=\W_t\cdots\W_1$,
$\W_s=\sum_v a_{sv}\mathbf P_v$, $\mathbf P_v=\mathbf D_v^{-1}\mathbf K_v$, and reads its truncated SVD
$\W=\mathbf U\boldsymbol\Sigma\mathbf V^\top$, $\Psi=\mathbf U_{:,1:k}\boldsymbol\Sigma_{1:k}$
(transductive).

\paragraph{Unified target.}
Let $\bp$ be the stationary distribution of $\W$ ($\W^\top\bp=\bp$, $\Pii=\mathrm{diag}(\bp)$) and
$\A=\Pii^{1/2}\W\Pii^{-1/2}$ its symmetrisation, whose top singular vector is $\sqrt{\bp}$. The
diffusion-map embedding is the top-$k$ factor of
\begin{equation}
\mathbf G=\A\A^\top-\sqrt{\bp}\sqrt{\bp}^\top=\textstyle\sum_{l\ge1}\sigma_l^2\,\mathbf u_l\mathbf u_l^\top .
\end{equation}
This $\mathbf G$ is the MDT analogue of DDM's $\A^{2t}-\sqrt{\bp}\sqrt{\bp}^\top$: since $\W$ is
asymmetric, the symmetric power $\A^{2t}$ is replaced by $\A\A^\top$. Each scenario changes only what
is \emph{fixed} vs \emph{learned} and the objective (Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}).

\section{Results}

\subsection{Accuracy: the encoder only matches the SVD (a ceiling)}
The most seductive early finding was a parametric ``neural SVD'' beating classical eig by up to
$+0.20$ AMI. It was an artifact of the \emph{decomposition}, not the operator or the network. MDT
embeds by plain truncated SVD of its operator; the $\sqrt{\bp}$ symmetrisation is DDM's out-of-sample
machinery, not MDT's, and plays no role here. The $+0.20$ baseline instead used a $\sqrt{\bp}$-weighted
\emph{dense} eig. Holding the decomposition fixed at truncated \texttt{svds} (Table~\ref{tab:confound}),
every operator variant---a random trajectory, an unsupervised best-of-eight, the oracle best-of-eight,
and the neural reproduction---scores within $0.03$ ($0.77$--$0.81$). Trajectory \emph{selection} adds
nothing once fair: a spectral-energy selector ties a random trajectory (regret to the oracle
$0.01$--$0.03$), because the near-uniform spectrum makes the eight trajectories nearly equivalent. The
network merely reproduces the SVD it approximates ($\text{neural}\!\approx\!\text{eig}_{\text{best}}$).
The operator itself still helps---features-only $k$-means is far weaker ($0.45$ on MNIST-10k)---so
diffusion contributes, but neither the selection nor the network does.

\subsection{Cost: a sparse truncated SVD is $\sim$300$\times$ faster}
The ``linear-time beats $O(N^3)$'' claim compared the network against \emph{dense} eigendecomposition
---a baseline nobody uses for a top-$k$ embedding. The correct method, truncated \texttt{svds} on the
same sparse operator action, is sub-second at $N=10{,}000$ and \emph{equally accurate}, while the
network needs $\sim$90\,s (Table~\ref{tab:scale}). The neural method is not a fast approximation; it is
a slow one.

\subsection{Out-of-sample (DDM's motivation): a wash within run-to-run noise}
\label{sec:oos}
This is the use case that justified involving DDM: MDT is transductive, so embedding new points needs
\emph{some} extension, and \texttt{svds} provides none. The contest is the learned encoder against the
training-free Nystr\"om extension of the same operator. A single-run comparison of a
\emph{Gram-matching} encoder (Table~\ref{tab:oos}) appears to favour Nystr\"om, but a \emph{multi-seed}
study (Table~\ref{tab:oos2}) shows this is not real: across five seeds, Nystr\"om, a regression encoder
on raw features, and a regression encoder on the affinity row all \emph{tie within a large run-to-run
variance} ($\sigma\!\approx\!0.06$--$0.10$ on the small/heterogeneous sets). Any single-seed ``win'' or
``loss''---including an earlier $+0.12$ for the encoder on MSRC---is inside that noise.

Two controls explain the non-result. (i)~A sample-size sweep gave a \emph{flat} gap over
$N_{\text{train}}\!\in\![200,1400]$, ruling out sample size. (ii)~The affinity-row encoder reproduces
Nystr\"om almost exactly---as expected, since with that input the optimal map \emph{is} Nystr\"om's
linear projection. Where heterogeneous-view data does hurt (Wikipedia), a per-view ablation shows the
culprit is \emph{fusion}, not the extension method: the text view alone clusters at $0.51$, the image
view at $0.05$, and fusing them \emph{dilutes} to $0.37$ for every method. The honest verdict:
\textbf{Nystr\"om is never beaten}. The encoder---whether Gram-matching or regression, both
multi-seeded---\emph{ties} Nystr\"om where features are informative (MSRC, Handwritten: within
$\pm0.01$--$0.07$) and \emph{loses} where they are not (Wikipedia: Gram encoder $0.29$ vs $0.38$, gap
beyond noise). It never wins; out-of-sample is a wash in Nystr\"om's favour, and all methods need the
train-set SVD.

\subsection{End-to-end: learning the geometry fails (and not the way one expects)}\label{sec:collapse}
Letting the network learn the per-view kernels and fusion weights under a contrastive objective
collapses clustering quality, well below a fixed random operator (Table~\ref{tab:collapse}). The
intuitive story---``the geometry collapses to a constant, gaming the loss''---is \emph{wrong}, as
instrumenting the training shows. Over 200 steps on Caltech101-7: (a)~the contrastive loss barely
moves ($58.36\!\to\!58.09$, ${<}1\%$), so the objective is \emph{not} being minimised---nothing is
``gamed''; (b)~the operator's effective rank \emph{rises} from ${\sim}1$ to ${\sim}850$ (of $N{=}1474$)
and the learned features \emph{spread} (std $1.9\!\to\!7.8$)---the opposite of a rank-one / constant
collapse. The real failure is one of \emph{optimisation and conditioning}: gradient descent through the
kernel$\to$product$\to$contrastive chain stalls, and the small drift it does make \emph{flattens the
operator's singular spectrum}, erasing the spectral gap that a top-$k$ embedding relies on, so AMI
falls. The stall is robust to tuning---sweeping the learning rate over $[0.001,0.2]$ and to 600 steps leaves
the loss flat ($<1\%$) in \emph{every} case---which is the signature of a flat landscape, not a
step-size problem. A landscape analysis pins the root cause: the $t$-step diffused operator has
entries ${\approx}1/N$ ($\overline{|W_{ij}|}{=}6.8\mathrm{e}{-}4$), so the contrastive logits
$\exp(W)$ are \emph{uniform}---$\mathrm{softmax}(W)$ has mean max-probability $0.00068$, exactly
$1/N$. The objective therefore sits in a dead-flat region, with vanishing gradients (fusion weights
$\lVert\nabla_a\rVert{\approx}10^{-5}$); no learning rate helps because there is essentially no
gradient. Confirming this, introducing a logit temperature ($\exp(\tau W)$, $\tau{=}20$) unsticks the
loss ($-8.7\%$ vs $-0.5\%$) and lifts AMI ($0.07{\to}0.13$)---so the failure is a \emph{logit-scale}
problem, fixable by sharpening but not by tuning lr. Even unstuck it stays below the fixed-operator
baseline, so end-to-end remains not worth it; we now simply know \emph{why}.
(Separately, in the \emph{imitative} setting the $\sqrt{\bp}$ weighting and a batch-normalised head are
needed to avoid the $\mathbf{F}{=}0$ saddle of the Gram loss.)

\paragraph{Mitigations, and why they do not rescue the method.}
The wash-out has an obvious countermeasure---diffuse less---so we test two. (a)~\emph{Short product}:
shrink the trajectory length $t$, keeping $W$ farther from the (uniform) stationary operator.
(b)~\emph{Laziness gate}: replace each step by a lazy walk $S_s'=g_s\,S_s+(1-g_s)\mathbf I$ with a
learnable $g_s=\sigma(\theta_s)\in[0,1]$, penalised by $\lambda\sum_s g_s$---a differentiable surrogate
for choosing $t$, since the integer $t$ carries no gradient. The $t$-sweep is striking:
$t{=}1$ is \emph{best on every dataset} and AMI falls monotonically with $t$
(Table~\ref{tab:collapse-fair}), often by a wide margin (MSRC $0.53$ at $t{=}1$ vs $0.24$ at
$t{=}4$)---the learned model always wants \emph{minimal} diffusion,
so MDT's multi-step machinery never helps it. This is the mixing-vs-hops tension: the well-connected
kNN graphs mix so fast that even two hops over-blur classes that are essentially a one-hop property.
Yet three caveats hold. (i)~\emph{Passive}: the contrastive loss still barely moves ($-0.1$ to
$-1.1\%$), so any gain is the operator resting near a structured single-hop/identity regime, not the
optimiser learning. (ii)~\emph{At least one hop is needed}: gating toward the identity ($\lambda{=}1$,
$\overline g\!\to\!0.16$, \emph{less} than one hop) collapses OutdoorScene ($0.072$) where a single
full hop ($t{=}1$) scores $0.34$. (iii)~\emph{It never beats a fair baseline}: against best-of-eight
trajectories (5 seeds), even the best learned setting ($t{=}1$) \emph{loses} on 3/4---MSRC ($0.53$ vs
$0.71$), Caltech ($0.23$ vs $0.62$), OutdoorScene ($0.34$ vs $0.60$)---and merely \emph{ties} on the
well-conditioned UCI ($0.867$ vs $0.854$, bands overlap). The apparent UCI ``win'' over
\texttt{fixed+random} was only against the single-trajectory strawman. The mitigations thus confirm the
wash-out mechanism and reduce learned geometry to a single-hop operator, but do not make it beat a
fairly-tuned fixed operator; the negative result stands.

\paragraph{Is the collapse a build artifact? A controlled kernel ablation.}
Our end-to-end model deviates from both references in a way worth isolating: it makes the kernel
learnable through a per-view feature projection ($\mathrm{Linear}(d,32)$, randomly initialised),
whereas MDT builds kernels on \emph{raw} features and DDM's network outputs the embedding
directly---neither projects kernel inputs. Two consequences follow. (a)~The \emph{random projection},
not the operator, drove the original collapse: re-initialising the projection to the identity
(raw-feature distances) and adding a logit temperature $\tau$ removes it---the \emph{untrained} operator
then matches fixed MDT on every dataset (Table~\ref{tab:collapse-ablation}, ``init''). (b)~Dense vs.\
sparse kernel is a \emph{wash}: holding init, bandwidth, $t$, $\tau$ and seed fixed and toggling only
the operator's kNN mask, the two move AMI only modestly and with no consistent sign (across 12
configurations), and \emph{neither collapses at init nor escapes collapse when trained}. Note the two
kernels are \emph{not} numerically interchangeable in general: on the low-dimensional views the tight
max-min bandwidth makes the dense Gaussian decay to ${\approx}0$ off-neighbourhood (the kNN mask keeps
$100\%$ of the mass), but on the high-dimensional views ($d{=}512,576$) the mask drops real
off-neighbourhood mass (only ${\sim}5$--$9\%$ kept). That the outcome is a wash \emph{even though the
kernels genuinely differ} is the stronger point. The robust,
kernel-independent conclusion: with a correctly-built operator whose untrained state already equals
fixed MDT, gradient-training on the contrastive objective still \emph{degrades} clustering (23/24
controlled runs)---the objective, not the kernel or the conditioning, is misaligned with clustering.
The faithful way to couple MDT and DDM is therefore the imitative encoder (Gram-matching a fixed MDT
embedding), not end-to-end kernel learning.

\subsection{What does survive (and does not need a network)}
Two minor positive findings remain, both classical and both cheap with \texttt{svds}; both held up
under multi-seed scrutiny better than the OOS or collapse claims did.
\emph{(i)~Consensus} (\emph{robust}): averaging the diffusion targets over trajectories beats any
single trajectory on all datasets, and over 5 seeds the gain \emph{exceeds} the run-to-run std,
from $+0.01$ (MSRC, $0.70{\to}0.72$) to $+0.14$ (100Leaves, $0.77{\to}0.90$); it also removes the
selection step.
\emph{(ii)~Selection criterion} (\emph{weak}): the silhouette index correlates with downstream AMI
somewhat better than the Calinski--Harabasz index of the original MDT, but the effect is modest---over
seeds, $\rho=0.55\pm0.20$ vs $0.40\pm0.24$, an advantage that sits \emph{within} the large run-to-run
variance, not the clean $2\times$ a single run suggested. Neither finding needs the parametric
machinery; both are operations on the SVD embedding.

\emph{A better internal-quality index.} Pursuing the selection question further, we compared three
label-free trajectory-selection indices on the canonical kernel over a pool of $30$ random
trajectories $\times 3$ seeds (Table~\ref{tab:iq}): MDT's contrastive Internal Quality index
(Eq.~19--20 of the MDT paper), silhouette, and a \emph{spectral energy} index---the fraction of the
operator's singular-value energy carried by its top-$k$ modes. The spectral index is the only reliable
ranker (mean $\rho=+0.23$ vs $-0.01$ and $-0.05$; the other two are uncorrelated with quality on this
protocol, so silhouette's edge over Calinski--Harabasz is itself protocol-dependent) and gives the
lowest selection regret on $5/7$ structured datasets. It is \emph{not} universal: on heterogeneous-view
Wikipedia-test (one near-useless view) it selects a high-energy near-degenerate trajectory
(regret $0.32$) where the contrastive index succeeds---the same fusion pathology that dilutes MDT
there. The gain is \emph{MDT-side, not DDM}: DDM's defining $\sqrt{\bp}$ weighting is inert here, because
the diffused operator's near-uniform rows make $\bp\!\approx\!$ uniform and $\A\!\approx\!\W$
(spectral energy of $\A$ equals that of $\W$ on every dataset tested). One property---near-uniform
diffused operators---thus explains three failures at once: the end-to-end stall (\S\ref{sec:collapse}),
the weakness of the contrastive index, and the inertness of the $\sqrt{\bp}$ weighting.

Table~\ref{tab:iq} uses the SVD embedding dimension $d{=}k$. Raising it is a \emph{mirage}: at
$d{=}64$ the selector metrics \emph{improve} (mean energy $\rho\!:\!{+}0.23\!\to\!{+}0.53$, regret
$0.12\!\to\!0.08$) yet the \emph{deployed} AMI (oracle$-$regret) \emph{falls} ($0.54\!\to\!0.52$ and
lower at $d{=}128$), because embedding into more coordinates of a flat-spectrum operator lowers the
oracle itself---a fourth face of the same near-uniformity, and a reminder that a shrinking regret can
track a collapsing ceiling rather than a better result. $d{=}k$ is optimal.

\emph{(iii)~Operator hyperparameters do not give a deployable gain.} Tuning the operator itself
(kernel bandwidth---global vs.\ self-tuning local scaling---graph degree $k_{nn}$, and
normalisation) has \emph{oracle} headroom (best grid cell $+0.06$ AMI over the paper kernel on MSRC),
but it is unreachable without labels: silhouette, which ranks trajectories acceptably, mis-ranks these
configurations, preferring near-degenerate high-silhouette partitions (on UCI a $0.80$-silhouette cell
scores only $0.17$ AMI), so unsupervised selection \emph{regresses below} the untuned baseline
($-0.01$ on MSRC). We report this because an early version, benchmarked against a kernel that dropped
the self-loop (lazy-walk) term, showed large apparent gains ($+0.18$)---another strawman baseline, now
corrected. Consensus remains the only deployable operator-level gain.

\section{Reproduction, external baselines, and scale}
The gaps a first reading flags are closed. \emph{Reproduction:} replicating the MDT paper's exact
protocol---kNN kernel with $k_{nn}{=}\lfloor\log N\rfloor$ and max-of-row-min bandwidth, knee-selected
diffusion times, \texttt{svds} embedding, AMI over 20 KMeans runs, full data---our pipeline reproduces
their Table~4: on 100Leaves we obtain AD $0.801$, ID $0.733$, MVD $0.814$ against the paper's $0.800$,
$0.738$, $0.824$. \emph{Field baselines (fair protocol):} under that same protocol the MDT consensus
embedding leads AD/ID/MVD/MDT-Rand on all three shared datasets (Table~\ref{tab:baselines})---but only
by $+0.04$ to $+0.08$. (A weaker re-implementation we used initially inflated this gap by crippling the
baselines; the fair numbers below are modest.) \emph{Scale:} on synthetic data the sparse-operator
truncated \texttt{svds} runs at $N{=}10^5$ in ${\sim}27$\,s where a dense decomposition is infeasible
($10^{10}$ entries, $O(N^3)$). \emph{Multi-seed:} every result above carries error bars. (Scope: the
scale test is synthetic---compute-feasibility, not accuracy at scale.)

\section{Lessons}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{The embedding's ceiling is the SVD.} A parametric network can at best match the top-$k$
subspace of its target operator; it cannot exceed it. Any reported excess is a baseline artifact.
\item \textbf{Strawman baselines manufacture progress.} A bad single trajectory and dense
\texttt{eigh} made the network look both more accurate and faster than it is. Truncated \texttt{svds}
on the sparse operator action, and a features-only control, dissolve the apparent gains.
\item \textbf{Out-of-sample is a wash, and single runs lie.} Over five seeds, Nystr\"om and both
encoder variants tie within a large variance (Table~\ref{tab:oos2}); the single-seed ``wins'' and
``losses'' (including a $+0.12$ for the encoder on MSRC) are noise. No extension is fundamentally
ahead, and all need the train-set SVD.
\item \textbf{Verify the mechanism, not just the number.} The end-to-end ``collapse'' is not a collapse
at all---instrumenting it shows a stalled loss and a \emph{flattening} spectrum, not the rank-one
degeneracy the AMI suggested. Asserted mechanisms must be measured.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
For embedding multi-view diffusion operators, the right tool is a sparse truncated SVD: it is faster
than dense decomposition, faster than the neural network by two orders of magnitude, and equally
accurate. The parametric and end-to-end variants of DDM$\times$MDT add cost (and, when learning the
geometry, instability) without benefit. We report this so the next attempt starts from the correct
baselines.
